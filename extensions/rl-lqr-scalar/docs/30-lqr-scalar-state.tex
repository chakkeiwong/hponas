\chapter{Scalar state and redundant-action diagnosis}
\label{sec:lqr-scalar-state}

\section{Question and matched controls}
RL-LQR-06 investigates whether a high state dimension is necessary for the
learning gaps observed in the preceding three-action experiment. Three
cases share the same attainable optimum: one state/one action (scalar1), one
state/three actions (scalar3), and the same scalar control problem with nine
irrelevant observed states (padded3). The scalar actor has two input features,
state and remaining-time fraction; the padded actor has eleven.

For $m=1$ or $3$, define $b=\mathbf 1_m^\top/\sqrt m$ and
\[
 x_{t+1}=0.92x_t+bu_t+w_t,\qquad
 \ell(x_t,u_t)=x_t^2+0.1u_t^\top u_t.
\]
The horizon is thirty, discount $0.99$, terminal cost $x_{30}^2$, initial
state an independent sign times $0.5$, noise a fresh sign times $0.025$,
and each action is in $[-1,1]$. Padded coordinates follow stable $0.8$ dynamics
with independent initial signs and shared scalar-sign noise, but do not enter
the active equation or cost. Sampling draws ten initial signs then slices,
so identical evaluation seeds pair the active scalar and noise in all cases.

\section{Redundant controls and exact decomposition}
Three controls cannot independently drive three directions in a scalar state.
Write $v=bu$ and $u_\perp=u-b^\top v$. Since $bb^\top=1$,
\[
 u^\top u=v^2+\lVert u_\perp\rVert^2.
\]
Only $v$ changes the state. The optimum sets the two redundant directions to
zero. Independent Riccati, condensed quadratic elimination and covariance
propagation give the common optimum $0.286662585467451$, compared with
$1.620099849071693$ for zero action. Row-wise sign-coefficient bounds prove
oracle feasibility. The effective feasible action range differs between
$m=1$ and $m=3$, but the common oracle is inside both.

With scalar Riccati curvature $p_{t+1}$ and optimal effective gain $k_t$,
\[
 J(\pi)-J^*=\mathbb E\sum_t0.99^t\left[
 (0.1+0.99p_{t+1})(v_t+k_tx_t)^2+0.1\lVert u_{\perp,t}\rVert^2\right].
\]
The first term measures incorrect effective control, and the second wasted
action effort. Full matrix cross terms and a pathwise martingale identity
are checked; small nonlinear fixtures verify expected cost by full enumeration.
The decomposition does not identify the training algorithm's causal failure
mechanism by itself.

\section{Tuning, comparison and interpretation limits}
The actor emits $m$ means and $m$ log standard deviations. Training uses its
squashed Gaussian; deterministic evaluation uses $\tanh(\mu)$. Twin critics
receive the state/time observation and all actions. Actor and critic layouts
are independently searched over two to four layers of eight to sixty-four
units, with separate continuous log-scale rates $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-2}$.
No oracle actions enter gradient training or distillation. Checkpoint contracts
reject cross-case replay and transfer.

Each case has paired procedure seeds 10601--10603 and four initial members.
BG-PBT and static search receive equal transition and component-gradient
minibatch work. Default results reuse one static lineage and have lower total
work. Selection uses 1,024 validation paths, seed1060001; all nine pairs freeze
before any16,384-path holdout outcomes with seed1060002. Normal95 intervals
are conditional test-path approximations, not uncertainty over optimizer seeds.
The once-only allocation is1,400,832transitions,347,328SAC updates and2,304
training-replay distillation updates (1,396,224component-gradient minibatches),
with four one-thread CPU workers and a coordinator. Equal component work does
not mean equal FLOPs. All unfavorable results remain reported.

Scalar3 versus scalar1 diagnoses redundant action learning but also changes
the automatic entropy target and stochastic policy dimension. Padded3 versus
scalar3 tests sensitivity to irrelevant observations while preserving the
cost-relevant model. Padded states are not the original ten coupled penalized
states. Three seeds and a fixed training budget cannot establish a general
causal explanation, global architecture optimality or convergence. SAC's
entropy-regularized training objective and deterministic quadratic selection
objective are distinct; objective mismatch remains a hypothesis to isolate.
