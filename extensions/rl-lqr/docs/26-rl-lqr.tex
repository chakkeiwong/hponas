\chapter{Known-answer control and joint SAC tuning}
\label{sec:rl-lqr}

\begin{decisions}
\textbf{Delivered scope.} RL-LQR-01 adds an optional, self-contained PyTorch
RLlib SAC example to HPO-NAS, including independent actor/critic architecture
coordinates, joint hyperparameter search, complete-path scoring and CPU/GPU
execution checks. The core remains qualified on its original source. This

\section{The model and what is tuned}
The fully observed scalar stochastic control problem has discrete time and
continuous states/actions:
\begin{align}
 x_{t+1}&=0.8x_t+u_t+w_t, & -1\leq u_t\leq1,\\
 x_0&\sim\operatorname{Uniform}\{-1,1\}, &
 w_t&\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\operatorname{Uniform}\{-0.05,0.05\},\\
 J(\pi)&=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{7}0.99^t(x_t^2+0.1u_t^2)
                  +0.99^8x_8^2\right].
\end{align}
The observation is $(x_t,(8-t)/8)$; time is needed for a finite-horizon Markov
policy. The last environment reward includes $0.99x_8^2$ once. There is no
state clipping or environment-side action clipping. This is not a
discrete-action benchmark and its noise is not Gaussian LQG noise.

HPO-NAS minimizes the unregularized expected cost of the delivered deterministic
policy, $u=\tanh(\mu_\theta(x,t))$. Standard SAC trains an entropy-regularized
soft value objective. The two objectives differ: the soft critic is not being
certified as the ordinary LQR value function. The objective definition is
frozen before fitting, and every reported cost uses that definition.

The actor architecture coordinate is either $[16]$ or $[32,32]$; the critic
coordinate is $[32]$ or $[64,64]$. The actor uses its coordinate for both its
encoder and distribution head. Each critic uses its own coordinate for state
and action streams, fusion, and scalar head. Thus these coordinates are not
literal full-network layer lists. Actor, twin critics and target copies have
separate parameter tensors. Four construction checks exercise every network
pair, forward shapes, independent coordinates and refusal of nonfinite tensors.

Two learning-rate recipes are crossed with all four network pairs: recipe 0
is $(\eta_\pi,\eta_Q)=(0.0003,0.001)$, recipe 1 is $(0.001,0.003)$.
Every network pair gets both recipes and fresh model/optimizer state. This
implements the joint problem; changing architecture does not inherit an
already-selected learning rate. It is an exhaustive eight-row Study with
three categorical coordinates, not a global architecture search.

\section{An attainable optimum and independent scoring}
Let $\gamma=0.99$, $a=0.8$, $b=1$, $q=1$, $r=0.1$, $\sigma_w^2=0.0025$.
With $P_8=1$ and $c_8=0$, backward dynamic programming gives
\begin{align}
 S_t&=r+\gamma b^2P_{t+1}, & K_t&=\gamma baP_{t+1}/S_t,\\
 P_t&=q+\gamma a^2P_{t+1}-S_tK_t^2, &
 c_t&=\gamma(c_{t+1}+P_{t+1}\sigma_w^2).
\end{align}
An independent exact rational calculation uses
$P_t=q+\gamma a^2P_{t+1}r/(r+\gamma b^2P_{t+1})$.
The attainable optimum is $J^*=P_0+c_0=1.078527$ (rounded).
The largest oracle action on any reachable path is $0.730304<1$.
Consequently the unconstrained lower bound is attained in this bounded-action
problem. This feasibility argument is essential: a generic unconstrained
Riccati solution is not automatically the bounded-action optimum.

All $2^9=512$ initial-state/noise paths have equal probability. Every saved
deterministic actor is evaluated over this complete law. There is no Monte
Carlo error in the evaluation expectation; training randomness and numerical
roundoff remain. The independent audit reconstructs trajectories, rewards,
terminal discounting and the performance-difference identity
\[
 J(\pi)-J^*=\mathbb{E}\sum_{t=0}^{7}\gamma^tS_t(u_t+K_tx_t)^2.
\]
The tolerance concerns floating-point identities only, bounded by machine
precision, horizon and magnitude. It is not a learning-success threshold.
Fifteen reference tests include rational/numerical agreement, direct one-step
quadratic minimization, the discounted SciPy DARE limit, policy-gap identities,
Gymnasium seeding/rewards, invalid actions and a wrong-terminal negative case.

\section{Frozen experiment and measured result}
Each fit receives exactly 4,096 transitions, batch size 64, 64 updates per
optimizer, replay capacity 1,024, training intensity 1, initial temperature
0.01, temperature learning rate 0.0003 and target smoothing $\tau=0.005$.
Tuning seeds 1101 and 1102 score every candidate. Selection is durably frozen
before fitting final seeds 2101 and 2102. Both phases use the same known law:
fresh seeds test retraining variability, not held-out dynamics or economic
generalization. All candidate outcomes, including unfavorable ones, are retained.

The selected $[16]/[32]$ recipe-1 configuration has final costs 13.049656 and
3.265531, mean \textbf{8.157594}; the no-action cost is 2.725760. The two final
optimality gaps are 11.971130 and 2.187005. Both final fits are worse than the

into scientific success. The small 64-update budget and training sensitivity
are practical limitations; the experiment does not isolate a unique cause of
poor learning. There is no automatic increase in budget to obtain a favorable
result. The search establishes only the best observed mean among its eight
candidate/seed/budget combinations, not the expected-seed or global optimum.

\section{Recovery and actual parallel execution}
HPO-NAS supplies Study selection, the durable evaluation/work ledger and
ResourceExecutor. RLlib supplies SAC's learner, twin targets, replay sampling
and optimizer losses. HPO-NAS is not built on Ray Tune: Ray Core execution is
optional in the core, while this companion specifically uses RLlib/Ray.
MacroFinance supplied read-only interface context; no sibling data, imports,
runtime environment or services are used.

The installed Ray 2.58.0 deterministic squashed-Gaussian conversion exposes
the pre-tanh mean. The companion overrides deterministic conversion to
$\tanh(\mu)$, preserving seeded stochastic actions and log probabilities.
Its catalog separates actor and critic architecture construction without
replacing the SAC training losses. No installed Ray source is modified.

The learner check performs 512 transitions, saves state, restores and performs
512 more. All four optimizers advance from 8 to 16 updates. Model and target
parameters, optimizer state, replay, replay RNG and temperature identities
match at restoration. The companion supplements RLlib's new-stack checkpoint
with the omitted replay and standalone temperature. Bitwise future environment
trajectories and GPU numerical reproduction are not claimed. A completed Study
restore returns exactly the retained result with an objective that refuses
calls, zero worker startup and zero extra transitions.

CPU repetitions use two distinct assigned cores, one numerical thread per
worker, with overlapping measured training intervals. GPU tests use two
physical GPU UUIDs, one device-local \texttt{cuda:0} per worker, actual CUDA
learner tensors and nonzero optimizer updates. The first hardware attempt's
training intervals did not overlap because setup durations differed. A reviewed
successor waits at a two-worker ready barrier after construction and times
training with CUDA synchronization; measured training overlap is 2.104343 seconds.
The predecessor remains a failure; its
1,024 transitions are counted. Total measured work is \textbf{76,800}
transitions, including the 73,728-transition joint study, 1,024 learner-resume
transitions, and two 1,024-transition hardware attempts. No speedup or
multi-machine fault-tolerance claim follows from the overlap check.

Other preserved failures were an oracle assertion using literal zero instead
of a floating-point tolerance, an overly long UNIX socket path (repaired with
an owned short alias), and a resume fixture that eagerly started its own worker
pool before asserting none existed. The latter is repaired by constructing
the executor without entering its eager context. No scientific fits were
repeated to repair Study resume, and no core defect was inferred from that fixture.

\section{User guide and artifact contract}
The source is \path{extensions/rl-lqr}; the guide is its \path{README.md}.
The tested runtime is Python 3.13, Ray 2.58.0, Torch 2.13.0+cu130, NumPy 2.5.2,
SciPy 1.18.1, with an isolated overlay containing Gymnasium 1.2.2,
farama-notifications 0.0.4, lz4 4.4.5 and ormsgpack 1.13.0. The runtime lock
retains wheel/file hashes. TensorFlow is not required. Add the companion
directory to the Python import path after installing the documented runtime:
\begin{Verbatim}[fontsize=\small]
from hponas_rl_lqr.reference import oracle_report
from hponas_rl_lqr.environment import FiniteLQREnv
print(oracle_report())
env = FiniteLQREnv()
observation, info = env.reset(seed=7)
\end{Verbatim}
For a separately allocated fit, use:
\begin{Verbatim}[fontsize=\small]
experiment.fit(config, seed, fresh_directory, transitions=4096)
\end{Verbatim}
The tested roster and declaration are exposed by \texttt{roster()} and
\texttt{declaration()}. Set \texttt{use\_gpu=True} only within the intended
worker device assignment. Each fit owns and shuts down its one-CPU Ray instance.
The registered checkout runner imposes four-core, two-GPU, 16-GiB RSS, 2-GiB
output and disk-floor guards. A standalone caller owns those operational limits.

Use the exact absolute commands in \path{docs/PROGRAM_OPERATIONS.md}:
\texttt{rl\_lqr\_ops.py} with \texttt{oracle}, \texttt{learner},
\texttt{joint}, \texttt{joint-replay}, \texttt{hardware}, \texttt{build},
\texttt{audit} or \texttt{master-audit}. They require the registered active
stage and reviewed manifest; consumed training is not automatically rerun.

Each fit retains \path{actor.pt} (standalone TorchScript inference),
\path{paths.npz} (all states, actions, disturbances and path costs),
\path{training.json}, \path{fit-result.json} and the learner/replay checkpoint.
Only trusted local checkpoints may be loaded: they contain Python pickle state.
\path{results-index.json} binds operation receipts. The final audit replays
saved actors and independently reconstructs scores, selection, prospective
freeze, budgets, checkpoint Adam counts and device intervals. Negative controls
alter scores, disturbances, actions, selection, freeze timing and GPU evidence.
All original chapters and consumed artifacts remain unchanged in this edition.

\begin{decisions}
\textbf{Explicit limits.} Known-answer controller gaps are measured, not assumed
small. Two fresh seeds do not establish statistical superiority or reliable
learning. Existing finite-MDP BG-PBT acceptance does not establish SAC replay
transfer, cross-shape distillation or actor/critic population search; those are
unsupported here. Broader architectures, environments, PPO comparisons,
economic effectiveness and production deployment are outside this allocation.
\end{decisions}
